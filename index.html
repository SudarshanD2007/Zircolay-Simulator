Mass and reaction estimates

The diffusion profile is a normalized single-region teaching calculation. It is not a validated multilayer oxide / α-Zr(O) / substrate solution, and its integrated concentration is not automatically equal to the stoichiometric oxygen uptake from the oxidation law.

Model, equations and assumptions

1. Arrhenius temperature dependence

D(T) = Dref exp[−Qd/R (1/T − 1/Tref)]

Here T and Tref are in kelvin. Dref is an effective diffusivity at the reference temperature of 1200°C. This lets you explore temperature sensitivity without claiming that one value applies to all Zircaloy microstructures or layers.

2. One-dimensional transient diffusion

∂C/∂t = D ∂²C/∂x²

The browser uses an explicit finite-difference scheme on a finite domain, with fixed surface concentration and a zero-gradient condition at the far boundary. The time step is selected to satisfy the explicit stability condition. The plotted profile is normalized and should be interpreted as a diffusion experiment, not a phase-resolved cladding prediction.

3. Parabolic oxidation law

x² − x₀² = k(T) · fsteam · t

k(T) is assigned an Arrhenius temperature dependence. fsteam is a deliberately simple pressure-dependent surface-supply factor. This is a teaching parameterization, not the original Cathcart–Pawel equation or a substitute for its published coefficients.

4. Reaction stoichiometry

Zr + 2H₂O → ZrO₂ + 2H₂

For reacted zirconium mass mZr, stoichiometry gives approximately 0.0442 kg H₂ per kg Zr. The reaction-heat estimate uses approximately 586.6 kJ per kg Zr reacted. In this interface, reacted zirconium is inferred from the simulated oxide thickness using a planar unit-area estimate and standard density assumptions.

5. Assumptions and limitations

  • Planar, one-dimensional geometry; no cylindrical curvature.
  • Single effective diffusivity; no separate oxide and α-Zr(O) diffusivities or interface partitioning.
  • Isothermal exposure; no coupled temperature field or heat-transfer solution.
  • Steam pressure enters through a simplified surface-supply multiplier, not a detailed gas-film or chemical-potential boundary condition.
  • No cladding stress, creep, cracking, ballooning, rupture, hydrogen embrittlement, fuel relocation, neutronics or radioactive release model.
  • No prediction of reactor power, core damage or meltdown. Fission power, decay heat and oxidation heat are physically distinct quantities.
For a defensible report, replace the illustrative oxidation calibration with coefficients transcribed and checked against the exact published Cathcart–Pawel source and alloy/temperature range you cite. Do not label these defaults as Cathcart–Pawel data.
